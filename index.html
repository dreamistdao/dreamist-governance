The monthly rhythm

One gathering. Ninety minutes. Plenty of space to be present.

The cadence is monthly. Adjust the time for the people in the room, and make space for a meal, a walk, or unstructured conversation around the agenda.

00—10 min
Open & check in
What changed this month? Every person may share or pass.
10—50 min
Member sharing
Focus, wins, lessons, roadblocks, growth, asks, and offers—about ten minutes per volunteer.
50—75 min
One Deep Dive
Slow down with one business or personal topic that deserves more than a quick update.
75—85 min
Group matters
Only approved proposals or improvements to how the circle works.
85—90 min
Close together
Name asks and offers, appreciate one another, and confirm the next host or facilitator.

A shared flow, not a performance

Bring it in your own way.

You can use slides, notes, a whiteboard, a demo, photos, or simply talk. About ten minutes is a guide, never a test.

  2. 02

When a quick update is not enough

Deep Dives make space for the thing that matters.

For the presenter

Own the next step.

Explain the situation and why it matters. Listen to what is useful, then choose what to do. The group is here to expand your view, not take over your decision.

For the group

Understand before solving.

Ask clarifying questions first. Then share relevant experiences, rather than immediately prescribing an answer.

Listen → clarify → understand → share relevant experience → let the person decide.

How we protect the room

Good conversation needs good boundaries.

These are meeting habits, not a rigid script. They make candid conversation feel safer and more useful.

“What is shared in the circle stays in the circle.”

  • Sharing and sensitive disclosure are voluntary. Listening and helping count as participation.
  • Ask clearly for the kind of help you want—and ask before offering advice.
  • Speak from experience: “Here is what happened when I faced something similar.”
  • No recording or sharing screenshots, notes, messages, personal stories, internal company information, or financial details without informed permission.
  • Take away your own learning, but ask permission before repeating another member’s story.
  • Challenge ideas, not people. No interruptions, comparison, flexing, or unsolicited selling.
  • Keep governance separate from confidential personal sharing, and periodically ask: Keep / Change / Try?

The closing principle

Come with something real. Leave with something useful. Help someone else along the way.

Dreamist DAO is an evolving practice. Keep what strengthens trust, change what does not, and make room for friendship throughout.
