# Decision-Making Rules

**Status:** Discussion Draft v0.1 · 20 October 2026

## Choose the right process
| Decision type | Suggested process |
| --- | --- |
| Casual activities | Volunteer organizer chooses within agreed boundaries |
| Reversible operating experiments | Consent-based decision with trial period |
| Charter or membership rules | Explicit group agreement under an adopted threshold |
| Spending, dues, or legal commitments | Separate written approval, budget and accountability rules before any commitment |
| Personal disclosures | Individual member controls what they share; never voted on |

## Consent-based process for reversible proposals
1. Publish a proposal before the meeting.
2. Ask clarifying questions (not speeches).
3. Hear reactions, with balanced speaking opportunities.
4. Ask for reasoned objections: is this unsafe or damaging to the group's purpose?
5. Revise the proposal or limit it to a small experiment.
6. Explicitly check each affected member's position; **no response is not consent**.
7. Record the outcome, owner, and review date.

**Consent is not enthusiasm or unanimity of preference.** It means no unresolved material objection to a safe-to-try proposal. If objections remain, do not label it adopted; revise, defer, or use another previously agreed process.

## When consensus cannot be reached
Document the unresolved concerns and revisit later. Do not improvise a majority vote or allow a founder to declare victory. Before any binding decisions, members must agree on quorum, thresholds, abstentions, conflicts of interest, and appeal mechanisms.

## Example
“Try a 90-minute monthly meeting for three months, with optional attendance and sharing; review anonymously after three sessions.”
