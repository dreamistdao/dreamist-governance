# Leadership Without Hierarchy

**Status:** Discussion Draft v0.1 · 20 October 2026

## Core principle
A leader serves the process; they do not control members' views. Formal roles rotate and do not confer superior voting rights.

## Suggested roles
| Role | Main responsibility | Suggested rotation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facilitator | Keep discussion balanced and invite objections | Each catchup |
| Proposal coordinator | Maintain board, owners, and review dates | Every 2 months |
| Community host | Arrange optional bonding | Every 2 months |
| Trust steward | Receive concerns, help arrange fair conversations | Every 2 months |

For a group of 5–8 people, combine roles as needed; no one should be overloaded.

## Facilitation habits
1. Start with the purpose and time boundaries.
2. Invite opinions before influential members anchor the discussion.
3. Offer quiet writing or an anonymous input channel for sensitive feedback.
4. Ask: “What have we not heard yet?”
5. Treat silence as unknown, not agreement.
6. Summarize opposing views fairly.
7. Explicitly ask for objections before decisions.
8. Check that no one is pressured to speak or commit.

## Leadership training exercise (15 minutes)
Pick a low-stakes proposal. One person facilitates; one observes interruptions, airtime, and whether objections are welcomed. Debrief the **process**, not personalities. Rotate roles next time.

## Power dynamics
Respected or outspoken members can influence others unintentionally. For consequential decisions, invite initial written opinions or let influential members speak last. These practices apply to everyone, not any named individual.
